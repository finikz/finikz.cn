Your attention is not yours.

From the moment you open your eyes in the morning, someone is already after it.

Algorithms want it. Notifications want it. Other people’s emergencies want it.

You think you are choosing to scroll on your phone.

You are not. Your phone is choosing you.

---

On May 5, a 22-year-old won the World Snooker Championship.

Wu Yize. From Lanzhou. He started playing at seven and went to Britain at 16. He spent those years in a practice room at the Crucible while other people watched short videos, hung out in group chats and collected followers.

Most people see a prodigy.

I see something else: during the most important years of his development, he happened to protect something.

His attention.

No one tells you this directly, so I will:

**From the ages of 16 to 22, China’s internet was at its most relentless.**

Douyin expanded until it became the air people breathed. Short videos compressed the average attention span to a matter of seconds. Countless engineers spent the best years of their careers figuring out how to keep you for another half-second.

Wu Yize missed all of it.

Not because he was especially disciplined, but because of his circumstances: the Chinese-language internet was far away, and entertainment popular with people his age was far away too.

His brain was never trained to need a new stimulus every 15 seconds.

That is his real advantage. Not talent. The way his brain was configured.

Here is a number you should know:

After an interruption, it takes people an average of **23 minutes** to get back into a state of concentration.

Twenty-three minutes.

A short video completes a full stimulation loop in 15 seconds.

You think you are “learning,” “thinking” or “creating” every day—but your brain is interrupted every few minutes.

You have never really begun.

You are always just getting started.

---

Most people misunderstand concentration.

They think focus is a personality trait, something you either have or do not have from birth.

It is not.

Focus is a muscle. Train it and it gets stronger. Ignore it and it atrophies.

For the past few years, you have been letting it atrophy.

It is not your fault—the system is built to help make this happen. The core design goal of every app is not to make your life better. It is to keep you for one more second.

Your attention is taken from you one second at a time.

---

This is not an article telling you to quit your phone.

That would be pointless.

There is only one question worth asking:

**When was the last time you were completely absorbed in something for more than two hours?**

Without checking your phone. Without switching windows. Without keeping another task in the back of your mind.

Just you and the thing you were doing.

Do you remember when it was?

---

Throughout history, the scarce resource has kept changing.

When knowledge was scarce, literacy was an advantage. When information was scarce, knowing things was an advantage.

Now information is not scarce.

**People who can process information are scarce.**

And processing information starts with attention.

Attention is the real hard currency of this era.

Not money, time or connections.

Attention.

---

After Wu Yize won the trophy, someone asked him what he planned to do next.

He said he would buy a house in Sheffield, keep preparing and keep playing snooker.

No “start livestreaming,” “get endorsements” or “build a personal brand.”

Just keep playing.

In 2026, that answer sounds like something an alien would say.

But that is exactly why he can win.

Most people’s problem has never been a lack of ability.

It is that their attention has never truly settled on the thing they are doing.

---

Distraction is free.

The system delivers it free every day, in unlimited quantities.

Focus has become a privilege.

Not a privilege you need money to get. It is a privilege you have to protect actively if you want to keep it.

Because someone is always trying to take it away.

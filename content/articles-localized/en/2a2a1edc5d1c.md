# Illegal French Lesson: Madame or Mademoiselle?

[![Illustration](/articles/images/da9ed5a506c00794.png)](http://p1.s.hjfile.cn/thread/201111/2011110732339836_931_o.jpg)

<http://www.madameoumadame.fr/>

A French feminist group recently launched a campaign calling for the word *mademoiselle* to be removed from official French documents.

The campaigners argue that men have traditionally used the word to flatter young, attractive unmarried women, and that it discriminates against women on the basis of age, appearance and private life—particularly marital status. The corresponding word *mondamoiseau*, meaning a young man, disappeared decades ago.

The campaign proposes using *madame* instead of *mademoiselle*. *Madame* is more respectful and makes no assumptions about a woman’s private life. A woman need not be married to be called *madame*.

The campaigners also want to retire *nom de jeune fille* (literally, “young girl’s name,” meaning a woman’s maiden name) and replace it with *nom de naissance* (“birth name”), alongside *nom d’usage* (“name in use,” often a married name).

Ladies, would you rather be called *madame* or *mademoiselle*?

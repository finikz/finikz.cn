### Céline Dion Sings Piaf in Tribute to the Victims of the Paris Attacks

*3 minutes*

Last night (November 22, U.S. time), Céline Dion sang Edith Piaf’s classic “Hymne à l’amour” at the American Music Awards, paying tribute to the victims of the Paris attacks.

I had spent much of the day watching ISIS propaganda videos on a website run by an American Middle East specialist who tracks the group. The videos were polished and manipulative, but the more I watched, the more I felt a deep, dark sadness. Then I came across Céline Dion’s performance on YouTube and quickly shared it, hoping to spread a little positive energy.

Piaf wrote “Hymne à l’amour” for her lover, the French world-champion boxer Marcel Cerdan. She first sang it in New York on September 14, 1949. On October 27, Cerdan was flying from Paris to New York when his plane crashed, taking him from her forever.

The Chinese lyrics were translated and adapted for singing by the veteran translator Xue Fan. It takes more than a literal translation to make lyrics fit the original melody. His celebrated work also includes “Moscow Nights,” “Auld Lang Syne,” and “The Straw Hat Song.”

### Music Biopics: A Few Familiar Formulas



## [Cadillac Records](http://movie.douban.com/subject/3005365/)

![Cadillac Records](/articles/remote/fef024a8f443960a.webp)

Books on rock history often explain that the term “rock ’n’ roll” came from a radio DJ who casually used the sexually suggestive phrase while playing a Chuck Berry song. Before rock ’n’ roll was named, these musicians played the blues. Among the best were Muddy Waters, Little Walter, Howlin’ Wolf, Etta James, and Willie Dixon, all signed to Chess Records, founded by Leonard Chess. He brought musicians and singers from working-class neighborhoods into the studio, where they made their names—and bought Cadillacs.

Even as rock’s “prehistoric” generation, these artists were direct and uncomplicated. They were not chasing celebrity for its own sake. Music meant lots of money and a new Cadillac; music was the American Dream.



## [Crazy Heart](http://movie.douban.com/subject/3182864/)

![Crazy Heart](/articles/remote/4cb4daf72dc0350d.webp)

No matter how glorious an artist’s past, inspiration can run dry and talent can fade with age. An unruly youth can turn into loneliness and exhaustion in old age. This modest film, adapted from a novel, has no spectacular set pieces or intricate plot. Country music accompanies a slow, delicate portrait of the inner life, leaving viewers with a pang: what we fear most is not loneliness but growing old without anyone to rely on.

A prodigal returning to the family is the most dependable mainstream value, wherever you are.



## [Gainsbourg: A Heroic Life](http://movie.douban.com/subject/3305739/)

![Gainsbourg: A Heroic Life](/articles/remote/7407fc8e3945df2f.webp)

The title may make you wonder: how can an artist who never fought in a war or saved lives be called a “hero”? In one sense, he is an ordinary person. But in art, every reinvention of tradition takes heroic courage—especially when it means rejecting and rebelling against oneself.

This film breaks out of the usual biopic mold. It blends fact and fiction and uses comic-book imagery to show Gainsbourg’s inner turmoil. The oversized face that follows him represents both prejudice about his Jewish identity and his own self-image. It is exactly what he wants to escape and destroy.

Gainsbourg went beyond ordinary reality. His eccentricity was a way to live in a more confusing, more artistic world—one that even the most romantic French people could not all understand. Avant-garde artists explore new ground without leading the way; they influence other artists.



## [Marley](http://movie.douban.com/subject/2996706/)

![Marley](/articles/remote/7ce059908aceea09.webp)

Without “the fastest man” Usain Bolt, many people would not know where Jamaica is. Without Bob Marley thirty years ago, many might not even have known Jamaica was a country. Born into a poor country’s slums, Marley had nothing but music. Through reggae, he helped prevent his country from breaking apart, encouraged African independence movements, and spread the ideal of “peace and love” around the world. This documentary tells the short, legendary story of his thirty-six years.

Bob Marley always seemed joyful because reggae makes you want to dance as soon as you hear it. He believed music, love, and tolerance could bring people together peacefully. He was not the only “dreamer” of his era. Do people still believe in that dream today?

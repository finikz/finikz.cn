## Cannes Classics

The full line-up for this year’s Cannes Classics has been announced. Focused on the year’s restoration work on classic films, the programme is also an important reference for selections at film festivals in China over the coming year. Save this viewing checklist for future reference.

Alongside the premiere of a new film by Bertrand Tavernier, a masterclass with William Friedkin, and tributes to documentary masters Raymond Depardon and Frederick Wiseman, the programme includes nine documentaries about cinema and 21 newly restored films. The latter include Krzysztof Kieślowski’s *Dekalog*, Andrei Tarkovsky’s *Solaris*, and the French epic *Indochine*. The full programme follows.

![Image](/articles/images/faac7ef0967d1ff0.jpeg)

### 1. Bertrand Tavernier

Premiere of *Journey Through French Cinema*

Bertrand Tavernier’s new film, *Voyage à travers le cinéma français* (*Journey Through French Cinema*), is a documentary about French cinema. Over three hours and fifteen minutes, Tavernier guides viewers through French films of the 1930s to the 1970s. The format recalls Martin Scorsese’s *A Personal Journey with Martin Scorsese Through American Movies* and *My Voyage to Italy*. This is one of the year’s most anticipated cinephile documentaries: a history of French cinema told through images.

![Image](/articles/images/abdc17981c8aaa78.jpeg)

![Image](/articles/images/7ca9d4f6e5c86db0.jpeg)

Tavernier said the film was a way to thank the directors, screenwriters, actors, and musicians who had shaped his life: “Memory rekindles; this film is like a small coal glowing on a winter’s night.”

### 3. Looking back at the 1966 Palme d’Or

#### A double winner

### 5. The first FIPRESCI Prize winner

#### Restored version of *Farrebique*

Georges Rouquier’s *Farrebique, ou Les Quatre Saisons* won the first International Federation of Film Critics (FIPRESCI) prize at the 1946 Cannes Film Festival. For its 70th anniversary, the film will be screened in a 2K digital restoration by Eclair.

![Image](/articles/images/67437713e6f42b66.jpeg)

### 6. Nine documentaries about cinema

As in previous years, Cannes Classics presents documentaries about cinema and its history. Since these are new films and little information is available, each is introduced briefly.

![Image](/articles/images/22fa68070a321f3d.jpeg)

#### *The Cinema Travelers* (2016)

Directed by Shirley Abraham and Amit Madheshiya · India · 96 min

A vanishing tradition, and a glimpse of contemporary Indian cinema.

![Image](/articles/images/b8c86db5bacc8382.jpeg)

#### *The Family Whistle* (2016)

Directed by Michele Russo · Italy · 65 min

The story of how the Coppola family came to America, its ties to Italy and to music, and of course its connection with cinema.

![Image](/articles/images/8ded706a8ab19c56.jpeg)

#### *Cinema Novo* (2016)

Directed by Eryk Rocha · Brazil · 90 min

A film essay about the Brazilian New Wave that is both political and poetic.

![Image](/articles/images/bda998abcc1fb1bf.jpeg)

#### *Midnight Returns: The Story of Billy Hayes and Turkey* (2016)

Directed and written by Sally Sussman Morina · United States · 99 min

Behind the scenes of Alan Parker’s *Midnight Express*, the film’s influence on the image of Turkey, and the real-life story of Billy Hayes.

![Image](/articles/images/ea581fe108c4ce9e.jpeg)

#### *Bright Lights: Starring Carrie Fisher and Debbie Reynolds* (2016)

Directed by Alexis Bloom and Fisher Stevens · United States · 95 min

The story of Carrie Fisher (*Star Wars*) and her mother Debbie Reynolds (*Singin’ in the Rain*), seen against both the sweep of history and the intimacy of family life. It offers a glimpse into two golden ages of American film.

![Image](/articles/images/be99e27102d1da3c.jpeg)

#### *Gentleman Rissient* (2015)

Directed by Benoît Jacquot, Pascal Mérigeau, and Guy Seligmann · Featuring Pierre Rissient · France · 58 min

A portrait of Pierre Rissient, a cinephile known in France and around the world. He helped bring many Asian films to Cannes, including King Hu’s *A Touch of Zen* (1970), which he brought to the festival in 1975.

![Image](/articles/images/ff8bb7ace05bd51d.jpeg)

#### *Close Encounters with Vilmos Zsigmond* (2016)

Directed and written by Pierre Filmon · France · 82 min

A portrait of the legendary Hungarian cinematographer Vilmos Zsigmond, who worked with many leading European and American directors. His films include *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, *The Deer Hunter*, and *The Black Dahlia*.

![Image](/articles/images/de2a8c4e9ee37857.jpeg)

#### *And the Woman Created Hollywood* (*Et la femme créa Hollywood*, 2015)

Directed and written by Clara and Julia Kuperberg · France · 52 min

The story of early women filmmakers in Hollywood, from Lois Weber to Mary Pickford and Dorothy Arzner—women who were later forgotten.

![Image](/articles/images/a6b5fada6b8f9d30.jpeg)

#### *Bernadette Lafont et Dieu créa la femme* (2016)

Directed by Esther Hoffenberg · Documentary · France · 65 min

Bernadette Lafont was a prominent actress of the French New Wave who worked with François Truffaut, Claude Chabrol, and others.

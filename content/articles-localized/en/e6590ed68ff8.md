# Merry Christmas, Mr. Bowie. Goodbye.

Originally published January 12, 2016 · Zhang Fengming

> David Bowie singing in Mandarin. In 2004, Hong Kong cultural figure and music critic Wong Chi-chung interviewed Bowie overseas and asked whether he would sing in Mandarin. Bowie immediately agreed. Wong asked lyricist Lin Xi to rewrite the theme song of a certain reactionary film as a Mandarin song called “A Moment Between Heaven and Earth.” Wong brought in Anthony Wong to record a Mandarin demo and teach Bowie the song line by line. The result appeared on the 2005 special edition of *Earthling*.

Last week, all kinds of Weibo and WeChat accounts were chasing the buzz around Bowie’s new album, released on his sixty-ninth birthday, January 8, and praising him for still being at the top of his game. After all, he was only 69, while older musicians such as Leonard Cohen and Paul McCartney were still touring.

![Illustration](/articles/images/4c4e6b2acc6c7375.jpeg)

But just after the weekend came the terrible news. The editor asked me, “Xiaofeng, have you written anything about Bowie?” I said no. He told me firmly, “Xiaofeng, you should write something. Bowie was a fine actor, too.”

![Illustration](/articles/images/1ee2942efeef6cff.gif)

Indeed, the artistic life of Bowie, the “chameleon,” was one long performance. Beyond the characters he created in his songs—Major Tom and Ziggy Stardust—and the various eccentric personas he played onstage and in music videos, he also acted in several films.

![Illustration](/articles/images/c70fd1629d791875.jpeg)

Let’s listen to Bowie’s breakthrough song “Space Oddity,” then look at two stories about film:

Many people think the song appeared on a hit album written to cash in on the Apollo 11 moon-landing story. Not so—it has to do with the great Kubrick, whom we were talking about just the other day.

Stanley Kubrick’s *2001: A Space Odyssey* came out in 1968. Young Bowie—he was not yet “Mr. Bowie”—was stunned when he saw it and watched it again several times. If he was chasing a hit, it was the film’s success he was following, not the moon landing. The film gave him enormous inspiration after a difficult time in music. The following year, at 22, he wrote “Space Oddity” and created his first alter ego, Major Tom, an astronaut lost in space. The BBC chose the song about space and spacecraft as background music for its live coverage of the moon landing. “They must not have listened carefully to the lyrics, haha.”

![Illustration](/articles/images/fb6991037b9ac863.jpeg)

In 1971, Kubrick made his masterpiece *A Clockwork Orange*. Bowie was inspired again and invented the androgynous, flamboyant Ziggy Stardust.

![Illustration](/articles/images/f2aa7e3a21307b40.jpg)

*Duncan Jones and David Bowie*

Finally, the actor Bowie left the world a fine director: his son, Duncan Jones. Duncan’s debut was the low-budget gem *Moon*, which true film fans have probably heard of. It, too, is about space.

![Illustration](/articles/images/2f2f6ffa2dabdc11.jpeg)

He went on to make the science-fiction film *Source Code*. This year, there is also *Warcraft*, which many fans of the game are surely looking forward to.

![Illustration](/articles/images/dfb359e38739cbfd.jpeg)

Bowie is gone. May he watch over Duncan from above and help him become as brilliant as his father.

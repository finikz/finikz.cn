# Four-Week Content and Pricing Plan

Originally, the plan called for thirteen posts over three months. On 2026-08-16, Finikz decided to compress it into a one-month rapid validation. What was compressed was not the content density but **the time it would take to make an offer**—from week twelve to week three.

## Core positioning

> **Train clients to use AI themselves.**

This one sentence draws two lines at once: unlike a teacher who talks about concepts and then leaves students unable to put them into practice, and unlike a content subcontractor who takes on tasks, this offer delivers capability, not copy. It is inherently an asset-based proposition: the client receives something they can keep using.

## Underlying setup

- **Reference framework**: The five capabilities in Sun Yat-sen University Lingnan College’s AI CEO II program are a checklist for spotting gaps, not a table of contents. Each article topic should grow out of something Finikz has actually done.
- **Primary distribution channel**: WeChat Moments. The target audience is “business owners I know, but not very well”; they are not on the official account’s subscriber list.
- **The official account is not a channel; it is a landing page** (confirmed in a test on 2026-08-18; see the internal note on official accounts as landing pages). The account delivered 1,642 messages, but only 7–14 people opened each article from their message list; 76–94% of reads came from shares. **Posting to Moments is the distribution action**; publishing on the official account simply puts the landing page in place.
- **Add one personal judgment to each Moments post**, rather than tossing out a link. That judgment is where people get to know him in a new way.
- **Every article needs one sentence people can repeat verbatim.** That is the fuel for sharing. Evidence: article 2 was shared nine times on the strength of “AI is not a tool; it is an employee.”
- **Write in the first person about how you do the work; do not analyze other people’s problems in the third person.** The former generates shares and follows; the latter does not (see the data below).
- **Litong project (started in August 2026, only two weeks in so far)**: For the first four weeks, mention it in one sentence only: “We took on a project, use AI to assist with most of the work, and are helping the client train their own people to use AI.” Do not go into detail until data and workflows have taken shape; then write a substantive case study.

## Four-week schedule

### Week 1 · Establish the position ✅ Complete (finished in two days, 2026-08-17 to 08-18)

**2. “There Are Two Kinds of AI Users: Bosses and Operators”** (published first, 2026-08-17)
- Draft: “I Have Been a Translator for Twenty Years—AI Is My Employee.”
- All the material comes from personal experience, with no client-confidentiality risk. Translation was the first industry threatened when AI arrived. He did not oppose it; he turned it into his own production line.
- **Data (official account dashboard, 08-18)**: WeChat Official Account: 81 reads / 9 shares / **4 new followers** / 51.85% completion / ¥5 in tips. Xiaohongshu: 664 impressions / 25 views / 4.10% cover click-through rate / 3 saves / 16-second average viewing time.
- **This performed best of the five pieces.** It led in completion rate, shares and new followers, and was the only one to gain a follower.
- **The first payment came in**—former colleague Yang Shuang, a video editor, tipped ¥5. Treat it as an intensified form of polite feedback, not a demand signal. But it proves that the article’s appeal extends beyond translators to people who earn a living from a craft and now feel AI closing in.
- It was shared nine times because of the line “**AI is not a tool; it is an employee.**” A judgment people can repeat verbatim is fuel for sharing.

**1. “Why Bosses Learn AI, Then Still Cannot Put It to Work”** (published second, 2026-08-18)
- Draft: “Why the Boss Learned AI but Still Cannot Use It Back at Work.” 1,368 Chinese characters, Stripe Docs layout, one cover and three illustrations.
- Positioning article. It names no course or school (the circles are too close; alumni may have enrolled).
- Main idea: **To be the boss of AI, you have to be able to say what you want, and that depends on being there on the ground.** Many bosses cannot use AI not because they cannot learn, but because they have no real use case in hand.
- Finikz added the word “boss” to the title, making the subject explicit: the readers are the people who pay, not the employees sent to class.
- Together with article 2, it forms a sequence: article 2 says, “You can be the boss of AI”; article 1 says, “Being the boss is not that easy—you have to be on the ground first.”
- **Data (official account dashboard, 08-18)**: WeChat Official Account: 60 reads / 3 shares / 0 new followers / 40% completion. Xiaohongshu: 599 impressions / 41 views / **6.90% cover click-through rate** / 2 shares / **27-second average viewing time**.
- **It performed worse than article 2**: 26% fewer reads, a completion rate 12 percentage points lower, one-third as many shares and no new followers. The reason was not quality but point of view: article 2 was “my story”; this one was “your problem.” A boss will not share an article saying other people have a problem, while an employee may feel awkward sharing it.
- On Xiaohongshu, this was actually the strongest item in the batch: its 27-second average viewing time tied for first, its 4.9% share rate was the highest, and the cover click-through rate rose from 4.10% to 6.90%—**the Finikz comic cover works**.

### Week 2 · Build trust (two drafts completed on 2026-08-18, awaiting Finikz’s edits)

**3. “The Boss Says ‘We Need to Adopt AI Too.’ There Is No Problem in That Sentence.”** — 1,360 Chinese characters
- Material: the opening line clients keep using when Zhishen AI prepares proposals, and how to translate it into a real problem.
- Load-bearing line: **Mention AI, and people start imagining; leave AI out, and people start remembering. Imagination is made up; memory is real.**
- Ask three questions without mentioning AI (frequency / who does it and for how long / what happens when it goes wrong), then use **countability** as the test for whether a task statement is actionable.
- Includes a section called “There Is One Kind of Client I Would Advise to Hold Off”—a public statement that the business would turn work away. **Before publishing, Finikz needs to confirm he is willing for Beto to see this section**, since it would effectively turn away some clients for the person handling sales.

**4. “Not Everything Is Worth Handing to AI. I Cut Three Things.”** — 1,381 Chinese characters
- Material: the translation workflow and the parts of client work that must never be handed to AI.
- Writing against the obvious argument is more credible. Managers are usually more interested in “what not to do” than “what to do.”
- Three things and three reasons: setting the tone (the text does not contain the information needed for judgment) / making the final call (AI bears no consequences, so it cannot make trade-offs) / saying “no” (the value was never about efficiency).
- Distills three screening criteria, paired with the three questions in article 3: the former identifies what to do; the latter filters out what should not be done.
- Repeatable line: **Doing every step halfway is the most expensive way to work.**
- The third point uses a client scenario, generalized with no identifying details. **Before publication, Finikz still needs to check that no one could recognize themselves in it.**

### Week 3 · Make the offer

**5. “A Client Asked Where to Start with AI. My Answer Is Just One Thing.”**
- Material: the Zhishen framework (see the internal documents on AI strategy and implementation and reusable modules).
- Core positioning: no tool training and no trend predictions; focus on the stretch between strategy and implementation.
- Core obstacle: the boss does not know which business process to start with.
- Answer: **start with sales and customer acquisition**—it generates cash flow most directly and makes it easiest to quantify what AI is worth.
- Delivery logic: diagnosis → use-case mapping → solution framework → standard deliverables. Customization stays only in mapping the client’s situation and making key judgments.
- **The most counterintuitive line in the piece**: the more customized a consultant’s solution is, the less trustworthy they may be. Customization means nothing has been retained as a reusable asset; every engagement starts from zero, and a large part of the fee pays for the consultant’s learning. The better approach is to apply proven modules to the client’s situation and use the time saved for judgment.
- This clearly separates him from a subcontractor who merely takes on tasks. It is also an idea he has already written down in his own documents, not something improvised for the occasion.

→ **Quote a price directly on Moments that same week.** (Format undecided; see below.)

### Week 4 · Close

**6. “One Person Plus AI, or Two People Plus AI?”**
- Material: designing the division of labor between people and AI. He is not teaching tools; he is designing how the work is divided. That is the real differentiation.

→ Send a one-to-one message to the people who have been following along, and close the deal.

Beto handles sales, reaching business owners through alumni groups and former clients. Payment is upfront, with no invoicing period. Target: deliver 3–4 sessions in two months (¥50,000–100,000).

**Actual delivery: 0 sessions** (as of 2026-08-16, the original two-month window had passed). Finikz described the obstacle this way: “I still do not have a clear idea how to do it. The hardest part is that I first have to give a free promotional presentation to attract sign-ups.”

So week three does not need a new product. It needs one sale of the product that has been sitting idle for six weeks, through his own channel. But first, one thing has to be decided:

- **Open enrollment** (individuals sign up; gather enough people) → requires a free introductory presentation
- **In-house corporate training** (one company buys it for its own staff) → no need to gather a group; only one boss needs to say yes

The two models have completely different customer-acquisition logic. See item 2 under “Known risks” below.

## Ownership settled (2026-08-16)

**Promote it under Zhishen’s name, with Finikz and Beto working on it together.**

> **Warning: This is exactly the setup that produced zero sessions over the past six weeks.** The original document on reusable modules (2026-06-30) already said, “Beto handles enrollment and pricing on the sales side.” In other words, “under Zhishen + sold by Beto” is not a new decision. It has been the arrangement for six weeks, with zero delivery. The name is not the bottleneck; the division of labor is.

**The division of labor must therefore name specific people and dates. “We’ll work on it together” is not a plan:**

- **Finikz**: six articles and the pricing post on Moments in week three. This is his own channel and his own network. **He does not wait for Beto or tie his progress to Beto’s.**
- **Beto**: business owners in the alumni network and former clients, on his own schedule.

> **Important: The risk to watch most closely.** Handing the act of making an offer to a partner is the most presentable form of “not asking for the sale yourself,” because it looks like a division of labor. If the week-three Moments post turns into “let’s wait for Beto to make progress first,” that is not collaboration; it is the old problem in different words.

**Agree on the revenue split before the first sale, not afterward.** Use the 2026 Litong project as a reference: an original quote of ¥5,000 per month was cut to ¥2,500, then later retold as “I was just helping a friend.” The same pattern could reappear in a partner revenue split.

## A note on content boundaries

Moments is Finikz’s own channel, so there is no problem with using his personal voice to promote Zhishen’s product. But article 5 (the Zhishen framework and diagnostic delivery process) belongs to Zhishen AI’s content territory—**when archiving it, do not copy it verbatim into the Fei Ni Ke Si official account**, or it will cross the boundary repeatedly recorded in the internal wiki: “The two accounts should not both eat from the same fish.” Articles 1, 2, 4 and 6 are personal experience and can go on Fei Ni Ke Si.

## Known risks

1. **No one will make the first move.** This has been verified twice (a comment-section prompt and a direct-message prompt; both got zero response). The week-three offer must be proactive. Waiting will not work.
2. **The assumption “we need a free introductory presentation first” may be wrong.** A free presentation is a way to build trust at scale when selling to strangers. Finikz is selling to people who know him somewhat; trust is already there. What they lack is awareness of what he does now—and that is exactly what these six articles are meant to build. **The content itself is an asynchronous presentation that does not require him to stand at a podium.**
3. **~~Capacity~~ Disproved by evidence (2026-08-18).** The original judgment was “one article every two to six weeks; six in four weeks is already a stretch, and I cannot add more.” In reality, the first two articles were published **within two days**. Writing speed is not the bottleneck and never was—the bottleneck is item 116. Capacity no longer needs to be treated as a risk.
4. **The knowledge-base topic has been removed at Finikz’s request** (2026-08-16). The system can appear as a tool at the level of method, but it is no longer the protagonist.
5. **Landy-related content has been removed at Finikz’s request** (2026-08-16)—that project is not “a free proposal”; the original framing was wrong.

6. **Zhishen AI appeared earlier than planned in article 1** (2026-08-18). The original plan was for the people on Moments to learn about Finikz’s connection to Zhishen in week three. In practice, he changed article 1’s “One More Thing” section himself to say: “Beto and I at ‘Zhishen AI’ have taken on several projects since the start of this year… We have been doing this for six months.”
   - This did not cross the “no duplicate content across the two accounts” boundary: that rule is about reposting content, while this is background about identity, placed after the main article.
   - **This is a good thing**: by the time he makes the offer in week three, Zhishen will not be four unfamiliar characters that appear out of nowhere. It will be a known identity putting a price on an offer, with much less of a gap.
   - **The cost**: from this article on, the author identity of Fei Ni Ke Si is no longer just “the translator.” Later pieces cannot retreat to a purely personal-work-experience voice.

7. **The official account cannot help make the offer** (confirmed by a test on 2026-08-18; see the internal note on official accounts as landing pages). Of its 1,642 followers, only 7–14 people open each article from their message list; the active core is fewer than 15 people. The week-three pricing post on Moments **does not need to wait for the official account to lay groundwork, and good-looking official-account metrics will not make the offer easier to sell**. The people who see the price are the people in his Moments network, and they have already seen two articles in a row.

8. **The writing constraints are now supported by data** (2026-08-18). First-person accounts of how the work gets done outperform third-person analysis of other people’s problems; every article must contain one sentence that people can repeat verbatim. Drafts 3 and 4 follow this. **Articles 5 and 6 must not return to article 1’s “analyzing the boss’s problem” approach**—it was the only one of the four to gain no followers and had the fewest shares.
